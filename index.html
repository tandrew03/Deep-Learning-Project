Robust Low-Bitrate Neural Speech Coding for Resource-Constrained Communications

A hybrid LPC–neural architecture for speech compression that stays intelligible under tight bitrate, latency, and compute budgets in accordance with the LRAC 2.0 Challenge.

📄

The problem

Neural speech codecs can deliver strong perceptual quality, but the architectures that make this possible are computational expensive and have high latency. On embedded systems and mobile devices, bitrate, latency, memory, and compute has to be controlled at once, and most learned codecs are only tuned for one or two of those at a time.

This project studies whether a hybrid design can do better: let a classical Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) front end carry the predictable structure of speech, and let a lightweight neural network handle the residuals.

Our approach

Each frame of speech is split into what a simple linear model can already predict, and what it can't. Only the redisuals part goes through the neural network, this keeps the learned component small.

01

LPC analysis

Input speech is split into short frames; LPC extracts the predictable spectral envelope and an excitation residual.

02

Neural residual encoder

A lightweight encoder compresses the residual into a latent representation.

03

Quantization

The latent representation is discretized to hit a target bitrate.

04

LPC synthesis

A neural decoder reconstructs the residual, which is filtered back through the LPC model to rebuild speech.

Evaluated across five bitrates

The system is benchmarked against the LRAC 2.0 baseline on clean, noisy, reverberant, and multilingual speech, at:

1 kbps
6 kbps
12 kbps
24 kbps
32 kbps
